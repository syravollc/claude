# Facebook group posts (engagement-first, no promotion)

Use one post per group per week, a different one in each group. Read each group's rules first. No links in the post. Reply to every comment within a day. If a post is held for admin approval, wait. Don't repost it.
All details are from your own story. Edit so it sounds like you.

## 1. Question: the hard part of year one
First-year homeschoolers (or anyone who remembers their first year): what's been the hardest part so far?
For me it was the freedom. At school the classroom told me where to be all day. Online I had lecture times and assignments, then I was free, and I wasn't used to it. I also missed my friends. It took about a year to settle. Curious what it's been like for your family, and I'm happy to share what helped me.

## 2. Question: what does your day look like?
What does a normal homeschool day look like at your house?
Mine was simple: I started at 9 and was done around 2, which left the rest of the day for other activities and things I was interested in. Looking back, I'd say the hard part wasn't the schedule, it was learning to use all that free time well. How do you structure yours?

## 3. Question: finding community
Fellow homeschoolers: what's the best way you've found community near you?
I thought homeschool social life didn't exist in my first year. It did, my parents just didn't know yet what to sign me up for. It took about a year to find local activities. After that I was in local high school clubs and orgs and church groups and had a full social life. What's worked for your kids?

## 4. Question: early college
Parents of teens: what's one question about starting college early that you wish someone would answer honestly?
I started college classes at 13 (I took the PERT test, finished high school, and enrolled). Rules differ by college, so I can't speak for everyone, but I'm happy to share what the process was like for me and what I'd ask the college.

## 5. Question: what did your kid tell you?
What's something your child told you about how they learn that surprised you?
My parents asked me what was working and what wasn't, and we changed things based on my answers. That ended up mattering more than any curriculum. I'd love to hear what you've learned from asking.

## 6. Tip: monthly check-in
A tiny habit that helped my family: a once-a-month check-in. Four questions:
1. What was the best part of the last month?
2. What was the hardest?
3. Is there anything you'd change?
4. Faster, slower, or the same?
Write the answers down and change one thing. Takes ten minutes. Do you do something similar?

## 7. Myth vs experience: socialization
The question every homeschool parent hears: "What about socialization?"
My honest answer as a former homeschooler: I had as much social life as other kids, maybe more. Local high school clubs and orgs, church groups, and later college clubs. It didn't happen by accident, we made room for it. What would you add?

## 8. Poll-style: online or in person?
Online instructor, in-person class, or no lectures at all: what works best for your child?
What worked for me was an online instructor while still working at my own pace. I didn't feel alone with the material and I wasn't stuck on someone else's schedule. Every kid is different. What's worked for yours?

## Don'ts
- Don't link, sell or mention your checklist unless the group allows it or someone asks.
- Don't post the same post in several groups on the same day.
- Don't share private details about your parents, brothers or other families.
- Don't promise results.
