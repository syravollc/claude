There's no single path. Here are the main ones I know about, with honest trade-offs. The right one depends on your child and your state.

## Option 1: Stay on grade level, build depth
**What it is:** same pace as peers, with room for projects and interests.
**Good for:** children who want depth, balance, or who aren't ready for more.
**Trade-off:** may feel slower to a child who is eager to move.

## Option 2: Subject acceleration
**What it is:** move ahead in one or two subjects (say, math) while staying on pace in others.
**Good for:** children who are clearly ahead in a subject.
**Trade-off:** schedules take planning; check how courses fit together.

## Option 3: Grade skipping
**What it is:** moving up a grade.
**Good for:** a child ready across the board, academically and emotionally.
**Trade-off:** it's a big step; gaps and social fit matter. Reversing it can be awkward.

## Option 4: Early graduation
**What it is:** finishing the required high school courses sooner. It's what I did, completing high school at 13 through FLVS Flex.
**Good for:** a child who is ready and wants it, with a clear plan for what comes next.
**Trade-off:** you must know what's next. Graduating early without a plan can leave a gap. Check diploma requirements and what colleges accept. [VERIFY]

## Option 5: Dual enrollment / early college
**What it is:** taking college courses while still in high school. See Chapter 7. [VERIFY rules in your state]
**Good for:** a child who is academically ready and mature enough for adult classes.
**Trade-off:** workload, schedules and age policies vary.

## Option 6: Online instructor-led courses
**What it is:** structured courses with an instructor, at your own pace. For me, an online instructor with my own pace is what helped most.
**Good for:** children who like structure but need flexibility.
**Trade-off:** some kids prefer live, in-person teaching, and that's valid.

## Option 7: Mix and match
Most families end up combining things. That's fine, and often best.

## How to choose

1. Start with the child: how do they like to learn (face to face with an instructor, online with an instructor, or without lectures at all)?
2. Check the rules where you live. [VERIFY]
3. Pick the smallest change that would help.
4. Try it for a term, then review.
