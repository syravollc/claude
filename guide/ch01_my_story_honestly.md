## Before we start

If you're reading this, you're probably a parent wondering whether there's a better fit for your child, or a student who has a feeling that the way you're learning isn't working. Either way, I want to start with the truth: **I'm not going to tell you that my path is the right path for everyone.** I'm going to tell you what happened to me, what helped, what was hard, and what I'd do differently. You can decide what fits your family.

## Third grade

In third grade, I got pulled out of in-person school.

It wasn't because I couldn't learn. It was because the way school was set up wasn't working for me. Learning in a classroom was one linear, one-sided style, and I couldn't adapt to it. It didn't fit how I learned, and no amount of trying to squeeze myself into it changed that.

My family decided to try something different. That decision changed my life.

## What changed when I was homeschooled

The biggest difference wasn't speed. It was **fit**.

- I could work **at my own pace**.
- I could study ideas I was actually **curious about**.
- I learned a lot **about myself**, how I learn, what motivates me, and what doesn't.
- And I had a **voice** in it. I gave my parents feedback on my experience, what worked for me and what didn't, and together we built the experience around me.

That last point matters more than anything else in this book. I wasn't just handed a curriculum. My learning was something my family and I kept adjusting, on purpose.

I finished my classes quickly because I could move at the speed that matched me. But please hear this next part clearly, because it's the thing I most want families to understand:

**Graduating early does not have to be the outcome.**

It was *my* outcome. Homeschooling can lead to many other places: more time to explore interests, a different pace in different subjects, a slower and deeper path, or a path built around something no school offers. Early graduation is one door. There are many.

## What I saw other families do

Over the years I've seen families go through the homeschool experience firsthand. Many tried hard and cared deeply. But I noticed a pattern: some families taught their children in the exact same linear, one-sided way they would have learned in an in-person school. The only difference was the room.

I understand why. It's what we know. But it means they missed what homeschooling can really be: an experience you can **adapt around the child**. For some kids that means face-to-face time with an instructor. For others it means an online instructor. For others it means no online lectures at all. There's no single right style. There's only the style that fits *your* learner.

(I'm not naming anyone, and I'm not judging. I'm describing a pattern I saw, so you can recognize it if it shows up in your home.)

## What "the right way" means to me

When I say homeschooling works best when it's done the right way, I don't mean a perfect schedule or an expensive curriculum. I mean three things:

1. **Build it around the child and their learning style.**
2. **Ask for feedback, and listen to it.** What's working? What isn't?
3. **Change things when they aren't working**, instead of repeating what you were taught.

That's it. If you take only that from this book, you'll be ahead.

## What this book is, and what it isn't

This is one person's story, plus what I've learned. It isn't legal, medical or psychological advice, and it isn't a guarantee. Every child is different. Rules vary by state and district, so check the official ones where you live. If you have concerns about learning differences or mental health, talk to a qualified professional.

## For students (a note to you)

If you're the student reading this:

- You're allowed to say what doesn't work for you. That's not being difficult. It's information.
- Try putting it into words: "What works for me is ___. What doesn't is ___."
- Tell your parents, calmly, and ask to change one thing. I did, and it changed everything.
- Hating the first stretch of a big change is normal. It was for me.
