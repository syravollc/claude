# Chapter 6: Records, Transcripts and Credentials

*Draft. Rules vary by state and by school; every [VERIFY] must be checked with official sources.*

---

Paperwork isn't exciting, but it's what opens doors later. Good records mean no scramble when a college, program or employer asks for proof.

## What to keep
- **Course list** with dates, what was covered and the grade or result.
- **Transcripts** from any school, online program or college.
- **Diploma and graduation records.**
- **Work samples** from key projects.
- **Activity log**, clubs, organizations, church, volunteering, with dates.
- **Test results**, if any.
- **Communication** with schools or programs about enrollment.

## Why it matters for acceleration
When someone is 13 and applying to a college program, the paperwork has to speak for them. Schools and colleges may ask for official records, and age can raise extra questions. Keep everything organized and obtainable.

My own path went through FLVS Flex for high school, Palm Beach State College for the associate degree, and Florida Atlantic University for the bachelor's. I can show my diploma, transcripts and degree proof, and you should be able to as well. [ADD: one thing you wish you'd kept better records of.]

## Check the rules where you live
Requirements for homeschool records, reporting, and diplomas differ by state. Look up your state department of education and your district. [VERIFY]. Don't rely on a blog, including this book, for the legal parts.

## A simple system
1. One folder per year (paper and digital backup).
2. Update the course list every term.
3. Save official transcripts when they're issued.
4. Keep a running activity log.
5. Back up everything.
