# Chapter 11: When Things Go Wrong

*Draft. This is general guidance, not professional advice. [ADD] spots should be honest stories from the author.*

---

Things will go wrong. That doesn't mean the plan failed. It means you're in a real plan.

## Common bumps and what to try

**The hated first stretch.** A big change can feel awful at first. I hated my first year of online school. For me it was a mix of things: no classroom structure, just lecture times and assignments and then a lot of free time I wasn't used to, and missing my friends, because I'd been pulled out mid-year without a real goodbye. It took about a year of settling in, and that was largely because my parents needed that long to find the local activities and groups that filled the social gap. What helped was time, an online instructor, and eventually the social side coming together. Give a change time, but check in often. If things aren't improving, adjust.

**Motivation drops.** Look at the cause: too hard, too easy, too much, no say, too lonely? Ask. Change one thing.

**A course doesn't fit.** Switch the course, the instructor or the format. Staying in something that doesn't fit isn't a virtue.

**Burnout.** Slow down on purpose. Rest. Talk to someone you trust. If you're worried, talk to a qualified professional.

**Falling behind.** Go back to the foundations (Chapter 5). Rebuild, then move forward.

**The child wants to slow down, or go in another direction.** That's allowed. Early graduation was my outcome, not the only good one.

**Social struggles.** See Chapter 8. Involve trusted adults. For anything involving safety or well-being, get professional help.

**Parent doubt.** Normal. Talk to other families, mentors, or a professional. Revisit the monthly check-in.

## A reset plan (use when everything feels stuck)
1. **Stop adding.** Hold new commitments for two weeks.
2. **Listen.** One honest conversation. What's the hardest part right now?
3. **Rest.** Protect sleep, play and friends.
4. **Pick one thing to change.**
5. **Recheck in two weeks.**
6. **Decide together** whether to continue, adjust or pause.

## When to get help
If your child seems consistently sad, anxious, withdrawn, or unsafe, or you're worried about their health, reach out to a doctor, counselor or other qualified professional. That's beyond what this book or I can do.

## For students
Having a hard stretch doesn't make you a failure, and it doesn't make your parents bad parents. It means it's time to adjust.
