# Chapter 5: Avoiding Gaps in the Foundations

*Draft. No research claims; general guidance only.*

---

Moving fast is only a gift if the foundation underneath is solid. Otherwise you're building higher on a weak base.

## What a gap looks like
- They can do this week's work but struggle when something earlier comes back.
- They memorized steps without understanding why.
- They freeze on problems that mix several ideas.
- A subject that "built on itself" (math is the classic) starts to feel suddenly impossible.

## Where gaps hide most
- **Math**, because each level depends on the last.
- **Writing**, where speed hides weak organization or grammar.
- **Reading comprehension**, where fluent reading can hide shaky understanding.
- **Study skills**, taking notes, managing time, asking for help.

## A simple gap check (monthly)
1. Pick a topic from a few months ago.
2. Ask the learner to explain it as if teaching you.
3. Give 3-5 problems or a short writing task, without hints.
4. Note what they can do easily, what takes effort, and what they can't do.
5. If something is shaky, go back, not forward, until it's solid.

Going back isn't failing. It's what makes fast progress possible later.
