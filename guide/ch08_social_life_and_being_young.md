## Part 1: Homeschool and social life
My honest answer: I had as much social life as other kids, maybe more. I was part of local high school clubs and organizations and church organizations. [ADD: one or two specifics, what you did, who you met, a moment you remember.]

It wasn't automatic. It happened because we planned for it. Social life isn't the thing that happens *in* a school building; it happens when kids have regular, shared time with others around something they care about.

### Build it on purpose
- **Clubs and organizations.** Many local school and community groups welcome homeschoolers, though rules vary. [VERIFY locally]
- **Faith or community groups.** Regular, familiar people.
- **Co-ops and classes.** Group learning with peers.
- **Volunteering.** Mixed ages and real responsibility.
- **Sports and arts.** Teams, bands, theater, lessons.
- **Friends of different ages.** Not only same-age peers.

### Questions to ask
- How many regular, repeating activities does the week have, beyond one-off outings?
- Does my child have at least one friend they can talk to freely?
- Are they invited and do they invite others?
- Do they seem lonely, or content with their own company? (Both are valid, but loneliness is worth noticing.)

## Part 2: Being young among older students
This is the part that worries parents most about acceleration, and it should be taken seriously.

### Things that tend to help (common sense, not research)
- A parent or trusted adult the student can talk to without judgment.
- Friends of different ages, including at least some close in age.
- Activities outside the classroom where age matters less (clubs, volunteering, faith or community groups).
- A plan for what to do if something uncomfortable comes up.
- Permission to step back or slow down.

### Things to avoid
- Pretending nothing is different.
- Pushing the student into situations they're not ready for.
- Treating loneliness as "just a phase" when it isn't.
