## Body

Hi {{ subscriber.first_name | default: "there" }},

I want to start by telling you something I don't usually say out loud: I hated my first year of online school.

I was pulled out of school in the middle of the year, which meant I didn't get to say goodbye to most of my friends. At school, the classroom told me where to be and what to do all day. Online, I had lecture times and assignments, and once they were done I was free for the rest of the day. I wasn't used to that. I also missed the social side so much that, for a while, I honestly thought homeschool social life didn't exist.

It did. My parents just didn't know yet what they could sign me up for. It took about a year before they found the activities and local groups that filled that gap, and things got better after that.

Looking back, a few things helped:

- **I could work at my own pace**, with an online instructor I could turn to.
- **I told my parents what was and wasn't working**, and they listened. At one point I told them I missed being in school and felt like I was missing a piece of my childhood. They didn't get defensive. They understood it was a process they were still learning about too.
- **We found community on purpose.** It didn't happen automatically.

If your family is in that rough first stretch, here's one small thing to try this week:

> Ask your child: **"What's one thing that's working for you? What's one thing that isn't?"**
> Write the answers down. Don't argue. Change one thing. Check in again in two weeks.

That's it. It sounds too simple, but it's the habit that changed the most for me.
